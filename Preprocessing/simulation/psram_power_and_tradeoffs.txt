1. THE CORE QUESTION: IS PSRAM MORE POWER CONSUMING?
================================================================================
Technically, YES — when evaluated as an isolated silicon component compared to
pure internal microcontroller SRAM:
  - Internal SRAM: True static latches (6 transistors per bit). Zero refresh
    current. In deep-sleep modes, retention takes less than 1 microamp (< 1 uA).
  - PSRAM (Pseudo-SRAM): Internally uses DRAM cells (1 transistor + 1 capacitor
    per bit). Capacitors continuously leak charge. An internal oscillator must
    self-refresh the memory array thousands of times per second, drawing
    between 20 uA and 80 uA even in standby sleep.

PRACTICAL REALITY:
In a portable device equipped with a display backlight and a GPS radio, total
system power draw is measured in tens or hundreds of MILLIAMPS (mA), NOT microamps.
In this context, PSRAM's power consumption is completely negligible (less than
1% to 2% of total battery drain).

4. THE "RACE TO SLEEP" ENERGY PARADOX (WHY PSRAM SAVES BATTERY)
================================================================================
In embedded engineering, total battery drain is governed by Total Energy (Joules):
  Energy = Voltage * Current * Time

Paradoxically, adding PSRAM can NET REDUCE total battery consumption during
trip routing due to the "Race to Sleep" phenomenon:

A. Architecture WITHOUT PSRAM (MicroSD Disk Streaming):
   - Entire road network cannot fit in 786 KB SRAM.
   - When calculating a route, MCU must repeatedly issue random sector reads
     over SDMMC to fetch node and edge records from the MicroSD card.
   - SD cards draw 30 to 60 mA during read cycles and suffer 1-2 ms seek latencies.
   - The CPU and SD card must run at full power for 3.0 to 5.0 seconds.
   - Energy burned:
       (45 mA SD + 20 mA MCU) * 4.0 seconds = 260 mA*seconds of energy.

B. Architecture WITH PSRAM (Memory-Mapped In-Memory Routing):
   - Entire road network resides in fast 8 MB PSRAM.
   - Cortex-M33 executes A* directly at 160 MHz with D-Cache enabled.
   - The exact same route completes in 0.15 to 0.25 seconds.
   - SD card remains completely powered down in sleep mode.
   - Energy burned:
       (15 mA PSRAM + 20 mA MCU) * 0.25 seconds = 8.75 mA*seconds of energy.

CONCLUSION:
Routing directly in PSRAM uses ~30 TIMES LESS ENERGY per trip search than
thrashing the MicroSD card to compensate for lack of RAM.

5. WHY PSRAM IS NOT USED IN EVERY PROJECT (THE 5 INDUSTRY REASONS)
================================================================================
If PSRAM is cheap ($1.50) and low-power, why isn't it on every microcontroller board?

1. Legacy MCUs Lacked Hardware Memory-Mapping (XIP):
   Older microcontrollers (Arduino ATmega, STM32F1) could only talk to SPI RAM
   via manual software commands (send address byte, wait, read byte). It was too
   slow for real-time code. Modern chips (STM32U5, ESP32, STM32H7) feature
   hardware OctoSPI engines with automatic address decoders that make external
   RAM appear as transparent linear memory at 0x90000000.

2. Random-Access Latency vs True Internal SRAM:
   Internal SRAM has 0 wait states (1 clock cycle latency, ~6 ns at 160 MHz).
   PSRAM is serial (4 or 8 data bits). A random access requires sending a
   command/address packet (taking 6-10 wait cycles before data arrives).
   * Note: The STM32U575's internal Data Cache (D-Cache) mitigates this by
     burst-reading full cache lines.

3. Most Embedded Devices Simply Don't Need Megabytes:
   90% of industrial microcontrollers run motor control, temperature logging,
   or CAN bus sensors. These tasks fit comfortably in 16 KB to 64 KB of RAM.
   Megabytes are only needed for:
     - Large color display framebuffers
     - Audio/video buffers
     - Spatial graph pathfinding (GPS Navigation)

4. Standby Refresh Physics (Coin-Cell Limitations):
   Because PSRAM is internally DRAM, it must continuously refresh its internal
   capacitors, requiring 20 uA to 80 uA in deep sleep.
   For products meant to run for 10 years on an unchargeable CR2032 coin cell
   (e.g. Apple AirTag or smart water meter), 20 uA is unacceptable.
   For rechargeable Li-Ion products (like your GPS), 20 uA is completely trivial.

5. Sourcing & Supplier Landscape:
   Standard DRAM and Flash are made by multi-billion dollar commodity giants
   (Samsung, Micron, SK Hynix). PSRAM is produced by specialized suppliers
   (APMemory, ISSI, Winbond). Some automotive/military corporations avoid parts
   that don't have 5 different manufacturing sources.
